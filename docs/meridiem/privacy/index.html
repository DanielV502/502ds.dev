Meridiem — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026
App: Meridiem, for iPhone and Apple Watch
Developer: Jose Daniel Valenzuela Morales · Guatemala · contact: privacy@syncthesis.io

The short version

Meridiem works on your iPhone and Apple Watch. There is no Meridiem account and no analytics. What you record, and what Meridiem reads from Health, stays on your devices.

There is one exception, and it is yours to choose: if your breathing therapy is followed on syncThesis.IO, you can connect Meridiem to it. Until you connect, nothing is sent anywhere; once connected, Meridiem reads your therapy nights from syncThesis.IO to show them beside your sleep, and, only if you allow it and pick them, uploads your therapy machine's files to it. It does not send what it reads from Health. You can disconnect at any time.

What Meridiem reads from Health

Only with your permission, which you give — and can change at any time — in the Health app. Meridiem reads:

  • Sleep: sleep stages and time asleep and in bed.
  • Heart: heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, the heartbeat series your watch records, and ECGs you take on your watch.
  • Night signals: respiratory rate, blood oxygen and sleeping wrist temperature.
  • Movement: steps, flights climbed, exercise minutes, stand hours, and your workouts — to say what you may be recovering from the night after.
  • Mindful minutes, and your date of birth — used only to pick the sleep-duration range for your age.

Meridiem writes nothing to Health unless you turn it on. The one thing it can write is the caffeine and alcohol you count in your Sleep Diary, saved as estimates, and only after you turn on Caffeine and alcohol to Health in You; any app you allow to read Health can then see them. With it on, Meridiem also reads the caffeine and alcohol other apps saved, so nothing is counted twice. When you breathe with the watch, it runs a short mind-and-body workout so it can measure your heart during the session; that workout is discarded when the session ends.

Health data is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared.

What Meridiem keeps on your devices

  • Your sleep plan (when you need to be up, how long you want to sleep), the city you choose, and your preferences.
  • What you note about your nights: the time you went to bed, how the morning felt, and labels such as "noise".
  • Your breathing sessions, and the heart readings taken around them.
  • CamCap captures: when you use CamCap, the camera reads the brightness of your fingertip to time each heartbeat. Only that brightness series is kept — no photo or video is recorded.
  • Alar-ME settings and a short log of each alarm (when it rang, how it stopped).
  • A diagnostics log of what the app did and when (the alarm, the link with the watch, errors). It holds no health data, notes or tags, and it stays on your iPhone unless you send it, from You › Send diagnostics, to wherever you choose.

All of this stays in Meridiem's storage on your iPhone and Apple Watch — and in a copy you save yourself, if you choose to (You › Your data › Save a copy): a file you keep where you decide, not encrypted, without anything from Health and without the syncThesis.IO connection. Your iPhone and watch pass it to each other directly. It is included in your device backups, if you make them, under your own backup settings.

syncThesis.IO, if you connect it

What it is. syncThesis.IO is a separate service, also made by Jose Daniel Valenzuela Morales, that keeps the data of breathing therapy machines (PAP) — when the machine was used, its pressures, leaks and the breathing events it recorded — for you and, if you choose, the clinician who follows your therapy. It has its own account and its own privacy policy: https://502ds.dev/syncthesis/privacy. Partner Connect is how syncThesis.IO lets an app you choose see part of your data, with your consent.

What it is for. Your therapy night next to the night Meridiem measured: whether and how long the machine ran through your sleep, with the night's figures from the machine one tap away.

How it is used.

  1. In Meridiem, open You › Connections › Partner Connect and tap Connect.
  2. syncThesis.IO's own sign-in page opens in Apple's secure sign-in window. Meridiem never sees your password.
  3. syncThesis.IO shows what Meridiem asks to read, and you grant it — or not.
  4. Back in Meridiem, your therapy nights appear under each night in Sleep.

What Meridiem reads. Your therapy sessions (start and end, hours used, the event indexes, pressures, leak, breathing rate), their summary, the machine's make, model and mode, syncThesis.IO's advisory insights, and whether your consent is active. syncThesis.IO identifies you to Meridiem by a pseudonym made for Meridiem alone, never by its own record of you.

What travels, and what stays.

  • From Meridiem to syncThesis.IO: the request for those readings, the access that you granted, and your device's model and system version (for example, "iPhone (iPhone14,5) · iOS 27.2") — so that in syncThesis.IO's My account you can tell your connected devices apart and withdraw one. Nothing Meridiem reads from Health, and nothing you note in Meridiem, is sent.
  • On your iPhone: the access, in the iPhone's Keychain, on this device only — never in iCloud or in a backup restored elsewhere; and the therapy nights read, in an encrypted file only this iPhone can open, so Sleep shows them offline.
  • On syncThesis.IO: each read is recorded in its audit log, as syncThesis.IO's own policy describes.

Disconnecting. You › Connections › Disconnect withdraws Meridiem's access on syncThesis.IO and deletes the access and the stored therapy nights from your iPhone. Your consent itself is kept and withdrawn in syncThesis.IO, where you can also see every app you have connected.

Uploading to syncThesis.IO. With your connection, Meridiem can also send files you choose to syncThesis.IO: the folder of your therapy machine's SD card, read through a card reader connected to your phone. Nothing is uploaded on its own: you pick what to send, Meridiem shows what is missing on syncThesis.IO, and only that is sent. The upload needs its own permission, which you grant in syncThesis.IO's consent screen and can withdraw there. What is uploaded is kept by syncThesis.IO under its own policy; Meridiem keeps no copy beyond what it needs to finish an interrupted upload.

Other permissions, and why

Permission Used for
Camera (optional) CamCap — your pulse from a fingertip over the camera. Brightness only; no photo or video is recorded.
Motion & Fitness Counting your first steps so that getting up stops Alar-ME, and noticing that you are up while it snoozes. Read only around your alarm.
Alarms A system backup alarm, in case anything stops Alar-ME.
Notifications If Alar-ME rings while you are in another app, one tap opens Stop.
Apple Music (optional) Waking to a song or playlist you choose from your library.

Meridiem never asks for your location. To draw the arc of the day, you name a city; only its time zone and position on the map are used, and they stay on your phone.

Deleting your data

You › Your data › Delete everything Meridiem keeps erases what Meridiem kept on your iPhone and disconnects syncThesis.IO, if it is connected. Delete Meridiem from your iPhone and Apple Watch, and everything it kept is deleted with it; if you install it again, a syncThesis.IO access left from before is discarded, and you connect again if you want to. What is in Health stays in Health, under your control there; what is in syncThesis.IO stays there, under your control there.

Children

Meridiem is not directed at children under 13. Meridiem itself receives no data from anyone; syncThesis.IO, if you connect it, follows its own policy.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date, before the release that needs it.
